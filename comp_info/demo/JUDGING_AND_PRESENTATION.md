# Judging, Presentation, And Evidence

## Five-Minute Presentation Structure

### 0:00-0:35 - Problem

- Museum visits are not equally accessible, personal, or understandable for
  every visitor.
- Introduce one strong piece of user/museum evidence, not several vague claims.

### 0:35-1:05 - Solution

- Define ATLAS in one sentence.
- Explain the headset, camera, Jetson, visitor profile, and conversational guide
  as one system.

### 1:05-3:20 - Live Demonstration

- begin the experience from the prepared ready state;
- identify an artwork;
- answer a visitor question in the selected language;
- answer a contextual follow-up;
- switch language or accessibility behavior;
- show one admin/monitoring feature only if it supports the story.

### 3:20-4:15 - Engineering And Innovation

- describe local detection, speech flow, knowledge retrieval, and response;
- show what the team built, measured, and improved;
- explain privacy and graceful failure.

### 4:15-4:50 - Impact And Business

- summarize user testing and measurable improvement;
- state museum buyer/user, cost structure, maintenance, and rollout path.

### 4:50-5:00 - Close

- repeat the slogan and the specific future ATLAS enables.

## Presentation Variants

- 30 seconds: problem, ATLAS sentence, one outcome.
- 2 minutes: problem, one live feature, evidence, impact.
- 5 minutes: complete judged version above.

Each team member must deliver every version and recover if another member is
absent or interrupted.

## Evidence Package

- test plan and participant summary;
- recognition confusion matrix and per-class results;
- response latency and end-to-end success rate;
- thermal, battery, and long-runtime results;
- visitor and museum-professional feedback;
- costed bill of materials;
- architecture diagram;
- prototype evolution and failures;
- privacy, consent, copyright, and AI-use records.

Do not invent survey precision from a small sample. State the sample size and
limitations plainly.

## Mock Judging Procedure

1. Give judges the report and video before the session.
2. Use a visible timer and stop the presentation at exactly 5 minutes.
3. Ask 5 minutes of unshared questions.
4. Score with the official Senior rubric.
5. Record missed answers, unclear transitions, and demo failures.
6. Repeat with a different judge group.
