# WRO Future Innovators Rules And Scoring

Source of record: the official documents in `../official/` and the live WRO Q&A.
This summary does not replace the official rules.

## Eligibility And Team Ownership

- A team has 2 or 3 students and a coach.
- Construction and coding must be done by the team.
- Coaches and other adults may guide and organize but may not construct,
  program, or build the booth for the team.
- Code must be written by the team or be readily available to everyone, such
  as open-source software.
- Violations can lead to a reduced score, loss of qualification, or
  disqualification.

### External Model Training Rule For ATLAS

Treat an outside computer owner as a compute provider only. The ATLAS team must
choose and understand:

- dataset composition and labels;
- preprocessing and augmentation;
- model architecture and size;
- training script and hyperparameters;
- model selection and evaluation;
- export and Jetson integration.

The outside person should not redesign, tune, label, debug, or choose the best
model for the team. Preserve scripts, experiment outputs, Git history, and the
team's evaluation. Obtain a written ruling from the Canadian National Organizer
because the published rules do not specifically describe borrowed compute.

## Robot Requirements

- The solution should use meaningful mechanisms, sensors, actuators, and one or
  more controllers.
- It must make autonomous decisions and do more than repeat a fixed workflow.
- Commercial components are allowed, but the team should explain why each one
  is appropriate and what the team engineered around it.
- All aspects must be demonstrable inside the booth.

ATLAS should demonstrate sensing, vision recognition, audio input/output,
language processing, user adaptation, and automatic decision-making as one
coherent robotic system.

## International Booth Rules

- Maximum occupied volume: 2 m x 2 m x 2 m.
- Three vertical display surfaces are supplied as close to the booth size as
  possible.
- Robot, table, people, and decorations must remain within the booth when
  required for judging.
- Optional table: approximately 120 cm x 60 cm.
- Up to three chairs are allowed.
- Fire and mist are prohibited.
- The live Q&A currently permits up to 1 litre total of safe, non-dangerous
  demonstration liquid, with protective covering. ATLAS does not need liquid.

## Mandatory Report

- English PDF.
- Maximum 15 MB.
- Maximum 20 single-sided pages or 10 double-sided pages, including
  attachments.
- Front page, table of contents, and source list are excluded from that page
  limit.
- A longer report receives zero report points.
- Bring at least two printed copies; ATLAS target is four.
- Identify every AI system used, its purpose, and the extent of its use.

Recommended ATLAS report evidence:

- user and museum problem research;
- prototype evolution and rejected designs;
- mechanical, electrical, AI, and software architecture;
- training data and evaluation methodology;
- failures, limitations, and negative social effects;
- real-world test results;
- privacy and safety design;
- cost, partners, revenue model, and next development steps.

## Mandatory International Video

- English.
- Maximum 90 seconds and 100 MB.
- Allowed types: AVI, MPEG, WMV, or MP4.
- The main content should show the robot operating, not repeat the report.
- Smartphone-quality production is acceptable; clear proof matters more than
  cinematic polish.

## Judging Format

- Each judging session is 10 minutes.
- First 5 minutes: team presentation and live demonstration.
- Final 5 minutes: judge questions.
- Teams are visited by at least two judging groups.
- Judges may consider conduct and independence throughout the event.
- The team, not the coach, communicates with judges.

## Senior Scoring: 200 Points

### Project And Innovation: 75

| Criterion | Maximum |
| --- | ---: |
| Idea, quality, and creativity | 20 |
| Research and report | 15 |
| Social impact and need | 10 |
| Key innovation and slogan | 10 |
| Entrepreneurship: cost, revenue, resources, partners | 10 |
| Next steps and prototype development | 10 |

### Robotic Solution: 70

| Criterion | Maximum |
| --- | ---: |
| Robotic solution | 30 |
| Meaningful engineering concepts | 15 |
| Code efficiency and software automation | 10 |
| Demonstration | 15 |

### Presentation And Team Spirit: 55

| Criterion | Maximum |
| --- | ---: |
| Presentation and booth | 25 |
| Technical understanding and quick thinking | 15 |
| Team spirit | 15 |

If ATLAS is entered in Junior rather than Senior, replace this section with the
Junior rubric before using it for decisions.

## Theme Fit

ATLAS primarily matches Area 3: Experiencing Art and History with Robots. Its
accessibility, multilingual guidance, and sharing of cultural knowledge can
also support Area 1: Protecting, Preserving and Sharing Cultural Heritage.
